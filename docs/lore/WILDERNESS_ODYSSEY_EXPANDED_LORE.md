**Status:** Companion reference — established detail, illustrative concepts, and unresolved questions  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Updated:** 2026-11-02  
**Authority:** `WILDERNESS_ODYSSEY_CORE_LORE.md` overrides this document when there is a conflict.

> **Read this legend before implementing anything:** **[Established]** means the idea has been agreed as part of the core world/story. **[Concept]** is an illustrative idea or suggested implementation, *not* an approved canonical fact. **[Open]** is deliberately not decided and must not be fabricated as settled lore. These labels preserve creative freedom while avoiding accidental canon creep.

## B. The energy experiment and what people knew

**[Established]** Clean-energy research first suggested a source or phenomenon beyond ordinary known physics. Dimensional research followed, accompanied by increasing secrecy and government interest. Researchers warned about unstable conditions, but leadership pressed on. The uncontrolled phenomenon involved both ignored warnings **and** an unexpected behavior the researchers did not understand. Their actions triggered the fracture; the meteor's passage created the sole true rift.

**[Concept]** The first controlled experiments may have produced increasingly severe power outages and readings that failed to return fully to baseline. Research logs can give contradictory views from cautious scientists, ambitious project leaders, and outside observers. Beal focuses on evidence and documents; Nathan gathers interviews and personal testimony. Do not finalize specific motives, responsible individuals, or exact physics until needed.

**[Open]** What was the meteor's ultimate origin? Was its arrival accidental, predictable, or intentional? What precise mechanism made the original opening self-sustaining? These remain mystery hooks, not promises that every question must be answered.

## C. The two waves and the 150-year Earth

**[Established]** The impact itself is immensely destructive. The second wave is not a second meteor or rift; it comes from material dispersed by the same meteor through the atmosphere, rainfall, groundwater, and food webs. Outcomes vary by organism, concentration, duration, and local environment. Earth's life recovers unevenly; some biomes remain hazardous, while others are thriving and lovely.

**[Concept]** Players might notice gradients of change rather than sharply dividing the world into safe and evil biomes: familiar forests, altered wetlands, contaminated streams, strange flora, recovered wildlife, and dangerous impact-adjacent regions. Existing Minecraft terrain can remain block-based; anomalous wildlife and environmental events should coexist with ordinary building and survival. A scientific field notebook or Aether scans may help players understand local hazards without turning exploration into mandatory exposition.

**[Open]** Exact biological mechanisms, list of adapted species, which mutations persist, global weather patterns, and geographical extent of the recovering impact zone.

## D. Project Eden: facility, selection, and survival

**[Established]** Eden started before the catastrophe as a hybrid program involving cryogenic/medical preservation, spaceflight, and emergency planning. Essential technical and scientific personnel were admitted initially; families and ordinary civilians entered as conditions worsened. Most occupants were evacuated; a smaller group remained. The player was an ordinary civilian whose medical condition became unstable after exposure to meteor material. Their chamber was an experimental and non-transportable installation. Aether chose to keep it operational after evacuation for reasons it can no longer fully explain.

**[Concept]** Possible Eden sections: administrative archives, maintenance and power systems, preservation wing, Aether's damaged core, restricted experiments, and connections to Reservoir-9's water systems. Players could restore lights, power, laboratories, and communications progressively. Introductory gameplay can teach basic exploration, scavenging, and simple repairs before the player opens the surface exit. Deeper wings may become optional return visits.

**[Concept]** Aether can restore archives with incomplete provenance, sometimes distinguishing confirmed facts from reconstructed hypotheses. It may ration power, suspend nonessential systems, or struggle with damaged memory. Strong dialogue should convey uncertainty without making Aether deceptive by default. The player can remember their own life; blanket amnesia is not necessary.

**[Open]** The exact exposure route from contaminated water into the body or preservation process; how long individual chambers remained powered; the other remaining occupants' detailed outcomes; why Aether prioritized this specific occupant; the technical reason the awakening signal prompted action.

## E. Reservoir-9: its identity and purpose

**[Established]** Reservoir-9 is an underground water/storage/purification and research complex, physically and institutionally separate from the original dimensional experiment. It provided critical water services and later researched anomalous contamination. Its infrastructure connected to Eden's systems; its archives may explain part of the player's abnormal preservation.

**[Concept]** A playable Reservoir-9 could contain storage chambers, treatment equipment, sample labs, restricted research records, environmental instrumentation, and evidence of difficult choices about distributing imperfectly treated water during shortages. Scientists need not be portrayed as foolish or evil; competing priorities can be understandable. Players should learn why the facility mattered through exploration, not a compulsory lecture.

**[Open]** Precise location, engineering layout, individual staff, complete contamination pathway, and whether every medical decision was known to Eden's leadership.

## F. Aether, the unknown signal, and Atlas

**[Established]** Aether is Eden's survival-support AI; its memory is damaged, and its preservation priority remains unexplained. The player's awakening is associated with an unknown-origin signal. Do **not** resolve these mysteries by simply declaring the signal came from Echo Earth, the meteor, Mars, or Aether's own original directive.

**[Concept]** The opening could feature garbled terminal output, rebooting emergency systems, a single working cryogenic pod, and Aether gradually recognizing the player. Aether can help catalog documents, analyze environmental data, and give context-sensitive exploration guidance. Earlier project brainstorming also mentioned an auxiliary system called **Atlas** going offline; its final role is not necessary to core lore and should be checked against actual code/docs before inclusion.

**[Open]** The signal's sender/purpose, the source of Aether's lost memory, exact power economics over 150 years, and the eventual extent of AI independence.

## G. Beal, Nathan, Oliver, and Peppermint

**[Established]** The journalists remain independent and spend the collapse traveling among survivor communities rather than becoming assigned operatives of Eden or Reservoir-9. They record blackouts, scientific secrecy, evacuations, environmental changes, and ordinary people's lives. Oliver is a dog; Peppermint is a pet lizard. All four reach evacuation boarding, after which the player initially has incomplete records. In the larger setting, Beal and Nathan's work is tied to Echo Earth's earliest human history and discoverable legacy. Neither appears alive as a present-day NPC, and the narrative should not sensationalize their deaths.

**[Concept]** Field recordings can mix disturbing events with warmth and ordinary details (finding food for Oliver, keeping Peppermint's enclosure warm, helping a settlement, interviewing families). Later archives on Echo Earth may contain their reports of arrival, new ecosystems, and early settlement life. Historical museums, statues, or peaceful memorials can honor them; discovering these can reward exploration without becoming a required final quest.

**[Open]** Which spacecraft they boarded; the exact journey, landing, and lives afterward; details of any eventual graves; how Oliver and Peppermint are remembered; where individual recordings survive. A memorial does not require a dramatic death scene.

## H. Mars and Echo Earth over 150 years

**[Established — Mars]** Mars is an advanced but environmentally harsh refuge. Technology and industry have developed enormously, yet daily life depends on protected habitats, energy infrastructure, resource management, and reliable life-support. Not all communities are equally prosperous or powerful.

**[Established — Echo Earth]** The original rift diverted late evacuation ships into an unfamiliar, Earth-like planet with dangerous and beautiful biomes. Human populations founded multiple settlements, some thriving, some struggling, some subsequently deserted. A loose inter-settlement federation coordinates shared research, transport, trade, emergency response, and potentially diplomacy; member settlements retain local control, and some remain independent. Residents disagree on renewed interworld contact. The council begins by allowing communications and science while restricting civilian physical travel.

**[Concept]** Distinct settlements may differ architecturally, environmentally, economically, and politically. Museums or reused landing ships can preserve early records. A representative council meeting can dramatize genuine risks: biological contamination, lost autonomy, dangers of the old fracture, and hopes for family or cultural reconnection. Relationships can separate personal trust from support for opening travel.

**[Concept, names are NOT canon]** Placeholder settlement examples previously discussed: a prosperous research center ("Haven Reach"), a cautious agricultural frontier town ("Mossgate"), and an abandoned colony ("Old Meridian"). Do not treat these names as approved or hardcode them without a design decision.

**[Open]** Map of settlements, exact size/population, federation charter, daily governance mechanics, first-contact dialogue, named political representatives, and whether/how Martian groups engage with Echo Earth's council.

## I. The single rift and gradual reconnection

**[Established]** The original fracture/rift became dormant after evacuation but persists as an untraversable residual space-time phenomenon. In rare conditions, weak information/transmissions can leak through it. Historic flight telemetry and recovered records provide early clues. Later unmanned measurements and probes support gradual verification. Reactivation, if pursued, must use old dimensional research, meteor material, and knowledge exchanged with surviving civilizations. The ordinary player helps organize/recover/build; Aether and other scientists supply expertise. There is no need to invent another rift or turn the player into a theoretical physicist.

**[Concept]** A safe research sequence: recover telemetry, rebuild a listening array, confirm artificial signals, collaborate with Mars, test small unmanned equipment under strict limits, establish Echo Earth communication, share scientific results, and assess whether limited transit can be authorized. Tests must not wipe a player's save, and a single cinematic shouldn't replace actual survival/gameplay progression.

**[Established]** The primary story milestone is reliable communications plus regulated scientific reconnection of **all three worlds**, not unrestricted civilian tourism. The Echo Earth council's initial restrictions remain meaningful. After the milestone, exploration and optional research can continue indefinitely; controlled transit can become a later unlock without contradicting the completed main arc.

**[Open]** Physical equipment used to stabilize the original rift, precise spatial coordinates, whether it is possible to move people safely, timing/terms of any council permission, and final explanation of the unknown awakening signal.

## J. Gameplay and storytelling guidelines

**[Established direction]** The modpack remains Minecraft first. Provide a memorable but reasonably short Eden opening; then allow free building and exploration. Story evidence should be distributed across ruins, environmental anomalies, research stations, recordings, quests, and helpful Aether interactions. Prefer a few memorable handmade locations with procedural supporting content over an enormous list of compulsory named sites. The world remains playable after the reconnection milestone.

**[Concept]** Suggested loose progression: **Survive** (Eden and base), **Discover** (Earth and Reservoir-9), **Reconnect** (old telemetry, Mars/Echo Earth communications), **Continue** (open-ended expeditions and settlements). This is a flexible production plan, not a rigid checklist or promise of every feature in the first alpha.

## K. Things we deliberately are NOT adding to canon

- Multiple **lore-level space-time rifts**, routine random cosmic fractures, or an Ancient City origin for the fracture. Vanilla Minecraft portals/dimensions and other gameplay travel systems can still exist if not falsely described as this unique rift.
- A predestined superhero identity or mandatory player profession/background.
- A fully specified origin of the meteor or the wake-up signal.
- Exact percentages of ships redirected to Echo Earth.
- An all-powerful centralized Echo Earth government or universal settlement agreement.
- Living present-day NPC appearances for Beal and Nathan.
- A violent compulsory ending for the journalists.
- Forced hard endings, world resets, irreversible save destruction, or post-quest gameplay lockout.
- Dozens of additional factions, secret programs, or lore chapters before gameplay justifies them.
